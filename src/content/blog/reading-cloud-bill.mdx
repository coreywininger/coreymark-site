## The core claim

Cloud bills are not accounting documents — they're operational
documents. Read as accounting, they're noise. Read as operations, they
tell you what your team did this month, what it cost to do it, and what
habits you're quietly subsidizing.

## The four categories I actually look at

```text
1. Committed capacity — reserved instances, savings plans
2. On-demand run-rate — what you're paying without commitments
3. Egress + networking — the silent tax
4. Idle + orphaned — the category that shouldn't exist
```

Most cloud cost tools show you twelve categories. Three of them matter
every month. One (idle + orphaned) is where the real money is, and it's
almost always a process problem — not a pricing problem.

## The three questions

Before I sign off on a month:

- **What moved, and why?** A 10% jump in on-demand is a real question.
- **What committed capacity are we underusing?** Reservations you bought
  and aren't consuming.
- **What's orphaned?** If it has no owner, it has no business running.
