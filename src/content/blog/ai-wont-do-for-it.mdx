## Why this post exists

Most "AI for IT" writing is either vendor marketing or doom. There's a
useful middle: an operator's view of what AI does well in an enterprise
IT environment *today* (April 2026), and what it quietly makes worse.

## The shape of the argument

### What AI will do

- Collapse the time between "customer pain" and "first plausible
  hypothesis" during an incident
- Turn runbooks from text into something closer to an agent you can
  delegate to — with guardrails
- Read a cloud bill faster than any analyst, and notice the boring
  inefficiencies humans skip

### What AI won't do

- Replace the judgment call on when to page someone
- Decide whether a change is safe to deploy on a Friday
- Own a vendor relationship
- Be accountable

### What AI will *move* (not eliminate)

This is where most orgs get surprised. AI doesn't remove the work of IT
operations — it moves it. Reviewing AI output *is* work. Prompt design
*is* work. Governing an AI tool your team is already using *is* work. The
headcount math doesn't net out the way vendors suggest.
